# GTM-360 Experience Layer (the Proprietary POV store)

> The moat. Research discovers what is publicly knowable; this layer holds
> what the operator knows that isn't in any dossier. Every entry is raw
> material for the Proprietary POV object (VOICE_SYSTEM.md §3).
>
> **Rules:**
> - Every entry is *yours* — a real observation, pattern, mistake, or
>   counterexample. Never a plausible-sounding anecdote.
> - Entries get an `applicable_tags` list so the engine can match them to a
>   topic at synthesis time.
> - `publishable` marks whether the insight can be written as the operator's
>   POV in public content, or stays internal (some scar tissue is too raw /
>   too client-specific to publish — it still shapes the thinking).
> - Grow it weekly. The calibration exercise (VOICE_SYSTEM.md §10c): take 3
>   operator pieces, annotate "this sentence is scar tissue because ___",
>   fold the patterns in here.
>
> **Linked from:** VOICE_SYSTEM.md §3 · consumed by Spec/Writer as the
> Proprietary POV object.

## How to add an entry

For each item, answer:
1. **The insight** — what you know that isn't obvious from the sources.
2. **The scar** — what happened that taught you this (the story, one or two
   sentences). "I've watched this fail three times; the official explanation
   was X; the actual failure happened six weeks earlier when nobody owned Y."
3. **The pattern** — the general rule a reader can take away.
4. **The counter** — what the mainstream narrative gets wrong, or the
   obvious opposite that's actually true.
5. **Where it applies** — topic tags (e.g. `territory design`, `OKR`,
   `content engines`, `AI in sales`, `pipeline hygiene`).
6. **Changed your mind?** — an earlier belief you held and why you flipped.

## Elicitation prompts (run these to surface scar tissue)

When adding entries, work through these — they're the fastest way to expose
what you know that research can't find:

1. **The "official vs actual" gap.** Where have you seen the public
   explanation of something not match what actually happened? (Best source:
   "we said X failed because Y, but it really failed because Z — six weeks
   earlier.")
2. **The three-times pattern.** What's something you've watched play out
   three or more times until it became a rule? (Three times = pattern, not
   anecdote.)
3. **The mistake ledger.** What's a decision you made that cost you, and what
   would you tell a younger operator instead? What did you believe that
   turned out wrong?
4. **The counterexample.** What does "everyone says" that your own experience
   contradicts? ("Everyone says territory maps should be geographic. The
   room I sat in failed because we optimized geography and ignored account
   quality.")
5. **Inside-the-room knowledge.** What happens in a GTM decision room that
   never makes it into a case study — the politics, the slide that stopped
   being believed, the real reason the deal stalled?
6. **The unsentimental explanation.** What's a situation where the honest
   reason is uncomfortable and nobody publishes it? (e.g. "the renewal was
   never about price; the champion lost the budget fight in March.")
7. **What you'd still believe with no citations.** If every article on your
   topic vanished tomorrow, what conviction would remain, and why?

## Publishing guardrails

- **Never fabricate an experience.** If the layer has nothing on a topic, the
  engine says so and writes analytically.
- **Protect the raw.** Entries marked `publishable: no` shape the operator's
  thinking and interpretation but are never written verbatim into content.
- **Client/people details stay out.** Genericize names and situations ("a
  portfolio company" not the actual firm) unless explicitly cleared.
- **Freshness applies here too.** Revisit entries yearly; a pattern you've
  since disproven becomes a `changed mind` entry, not a silent deletion.
